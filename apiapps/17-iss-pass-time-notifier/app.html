Quick Facts
🕐
ISS orbits Earth every 90 minutes
At ~408 km altitude, one orbit takes roughly 92 minutes, meaning about 16 sunrises and sunsets per day.
🚀
ISS travels at ~28,000 km/h
That's about 8 km per second — fast enough to cross the United States in under 10 minutes.
🏠
Continuously occupied since November 2, 2000
Humans have lived aboard the International Space Station for over 25 years without interruption.
  • Look just after sunset or just before sunrise — the ISS is lit by the sun but the sky is dark enough to see it.
  • It looks like a very bright, fast-moving star — brighter than most stars and with no blinking lights.
  • It moves steadily across the sky over 1–6 minutes, much faster than aircraft but not as fast as a meteor.
  • Use this tracker to know when it will pass your location. Check "distance to ISS" — under ~2,500 km means it may be in your sky.
  • No special equipment needed — naked eye is all you need. But binoculars can reveal its cross-shaped silhouette.
