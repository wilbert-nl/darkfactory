# SyntheticSummer — Research Brief

## What It Is
A wellness platform for people in cold or northern countries to virtually experience summer through beach ambience, warm light therapy simulation, tropical content, and seasonal mood plans. Targets Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD) sufferers and winter-weary users who want a sensory escape.

## Competitors
| Name | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Calm | Ambient soundscapes; $596M revenue (2024); offers nature sounds but no summer/tropical wellness theming |
| Endel | AI-generated soundscapes; $15.8M revenue (2025); not seasonally or climatically themed |
| Headspace | Mindfulness app; no seasonal theming |
| YouTube / Spotify ambient playlists | Free beach sounds with millions of plays; not interactive, not personalized |
| Tripp / Guided Meditation VR | Immersive tropical environments in VR; not mainstream, not mobile-accessible |

## Market Size
SAD light therapy market $1.6B (2023) → $2.86B by 2030 (7.5% CAGR) — direct signal that cold-country users pay for mood relief. Wellness tourism market $1T+ (2024), 9% CAGR. SAD affects ~5% of the US population; Nordic countries see 10–20% of residents reporting winter depression. Scandinavian and Northern European markets are high-paying demographics with strong wellness spending habits.

## MVP Features
1. Ambient soundscapes (beach waves, tropical rain, jungle, outdoor café)
2. "Virtual sun" mode — warm-toned light therapy screen overlay
3. Curated tropical content feed (photos, travel videos, nature clips)
4. Seasonal wellness plan (mood log, light exposure tracker, vitamin D reminders)
5. Background play while working with Pomodoro-style "summer mode" sessions
6. Relaxation playlist integration via Spotify/Apple Music embed

## Differentiators
1. SAD/winter-specific positioning — no competitor explicitly targets cold-country users or winter depression as a primary use case; a clear underserved niche vs. general mindfulness apps
2. Light therapy integration — combines warm light simulation with soundscapes and curated content for a multi-sensory approach that Calm and Endel do not attempt
3. Community and seasonal events — "virtual beach meetups," seasonal challenges, and user photo sharing from past summers create social retention hooks that all current competitors lack

## Profitability
**Model:** Subscription at $6.99/mo or $49.99/yr (priced below Calm's $69.99/yr). Seasonal pricing spike — promote heavily October through March in the Northern Hemisphere. Affiliate partnerships with SAD lamp manufacturers, travel brands, and vitamin D supplement companies as secondary revenue streams.

**Estimate:** 50K subscribers × $49.99/yr = $2.5M ARR.

## Build Ease: 4/5
Content curation and ambient UX are technically straightforward. AI handles mood journaling analysis, personalized suggestions, generative ambient soundscape mixing, and content recommendations. Main difficulty: licensing compelling tropical video content and building visual design distinctive enough to differentiate from a free YouTube search result.

## Legal Risks
- Medical claims risk — marketing as SAD treatment or light therapy may attract FDA/CE regulatory scrutiny; keep all claims in "wellness" language, never "treatment"
- Content licensing — tropical video and photo assets must be properly licensed; YouTube embeds are fine, original or curated content requires licensing or creative commons sourcing
- GDPR for mood and wellness data — mood logs and health-adjacent data are sensitive personal data under EU law; require explicit consent and provide clear data deletion options
